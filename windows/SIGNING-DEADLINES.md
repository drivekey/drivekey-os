# Signing deadlines and completed responses

The application deadline controls admission to a new signature. It cannot revoke an existing Ethereum transaction, Solana transaction or private circuit authorization. A worker must return its original completed response even when crypto completion or delivery crosses that deadline. The durable signing-attempt journals then retain it before further UI work or export. A timeout or process death remains an uncertain attempt; it does not grant a replacement signature or broadcast.

Finite-matrix chain signers capture `signedAt` at the validated signing-start boundary. Wallet review approvals are produced before that boundary. The original request is checked again, using monotonically advancing elapsed time, immediately before transaction signing. Final cryptographic self-verification uses historical verification (`expiry=false`), which still validates the exact wallet, request, bytes, signature, signing timestamp and every transaction invariant. It does not permit a new signature for an expired request. This covers v8, v9, v10, v11, v12, v14, v16, v18, v21, v22, v23, v24, v25, v26 and the v29 envelope. Historical pre-matrix formats retain their original read domains.

Circuit authorizers v13, v15, v17, v19, v20 and v27 likewise capture and validate their signing-start timestamp. The v28 product authorization may have an earlier deadline than its embedded seed. Its synchronous boundary callback therefore checks the unchanged original product request immediately before the inner EdDSA signature. The attestation key is unlocked beforehand. Once the inner signature exists, the outer wallet signature completes the exact product response and historical verification retains it. No deadline, seed, fingerprint or clock is rewritten to make an expired request current. The outer response timestamp equals the inner admission timestamp.

`tests/signer-deadline.test.cjs` uses fresh encrypted unfunded vaults and controlled recipients for all four payment asset/chain combinations, including Solana USDC account creation. It delays delivery after genuine crypto has created the bytes until a real short deadline passes. It also verifies that expiry during unlock or review approval produces no transaction. `tests/product-proof-deadline.test.cjs` uses a synthetic owned note and real EdDSA/wallet signatures. It tests delayed circuit and wallet completion and the earlier outer deadline. Test-only esbuild adapters delay actual key/crypto delivery; they are absent from product bundles. No network, protected signer, funding, submission, native approval or mainnet settlement is involved.

These checks are local engineering evidence. Current installed-mainnet cases, separate-device signing, positive native file handoffs, final independent review and release gates still require their own evidence. Preserved expired bytes must never be automatically submitted or rebroadcast.
